Scraedai (Cicadas)
The old wizard leaned closer to the cracked stone, his broad antennae twitching as a low drone built within his chest. "No, no. Hear that?" he asked. His companion stared at the wall. "I don't hear anything." "Exactly!" The Scraedai laughed, delighted. "You've been striking the wrong spot." He placed two fingers against the stone and hummed a single, impossibly deep note. For a moment nothing happened. Then the entire wall shuddered, a web of cracks raced across its surface, and several tons of ancient masonry collapsed inward. The Scraedai peered eagerly into the darkness beyond. "Now, what do you suppose they were trying so hard to keep us from finding?"

Formed of Song and Sound

It is believed by the Scraedai that they were not formed from lesser bugs like most races, as there is no wild bug resembling them; instead, they believe their beloved goddess Caelimynn sung them into being, pulling upon the resonant threads of magic itself to create something wholly unique. This belief is supported, at least in the minds of the Scraedai, by their natural inclination towards sonomancy.

To the Scraedai, sonomancy is far more than the manipulation of sound. Everything has its own resonance, from the smallest stone to the mightiest spell, and by understanding these resonances one can come closer to understanding magic in its purest form. Whether this is actually true is a matter of considerable debate among other arcane scholars, though few Scraedai sonomancers give such objections much consideration.

It has been centuries since Caelimynn was wounded in battle against the Maidoir goddess Gulnach. Though she rests in restorative slumber deep beneath their home within the Whistlewood, the Scraedai still hold tightly to their creator. Some believe that her injury left more than Caelimynn wounded, and that somewhere beneath the ordinary sounds of the world can be heard a subtle disharmony that will only cease when their goddess finally awakens.

A Culture of the Arcane

The Scraedai hold knowledge and mastery of the arcane over almost anything else. Every level of their loose society is built around magic, with almost every menial task taken care of through magical means. Enchanted tools sweep floors and tend gardens, magical lights illuminate homes and streets, and hollows carry messages, goods and whatever else their masters cannot be bothered to carry themselves.

This lack of scarcity allows almost every Scraedai to study the ways of wizardry in some capacity, with even their soldiers commonly following the ways of the eldritch knight. Those without the talent or interest to become accomplished wizards themselves are rarely looked down upon, but there are few things a Scraedai respects more than true arcane mastery.

Education is similarly loose. Schools and colleges exist throughout the Whistlewood, but apprentices frequently move between masters, attend public lectures or simply appear uninvited at laboratories where interesting work is being performed. Accomplished scholars often attract circles of students and assistants, with rivalries between competing academics sometimes continuing for generations.

"Good" Necromancy

While necromancy in Llorelan is usually tied with the loss of identity, corruption or the vile sacrifices of lichdom, the Scraedai, with their love of all arcane arts, have found an alternate path. The Scraedai regularly create undead out of their shed shells, called hollows, as servants. In fact, the practice is so common that giving someone a hollow is seen as a friendly gesture. Foreign visitors are sometimes less appreciative, especially when they realize the servant pouring their tea is the discarded shell of their host.

Beyond this, the most respected members of Scraedai society are actually a form of lich. Known as Grandmasters, they are individuals who have mastered the arcane so thoroughly that they are able to bind their souls to their bodies by creating a "song" that perfectly captures every aspect of their identity.

Most of the time these Grandmasters lay dormant, sitting dead upon an armchair or in front of a fireplace their servants keep lit. However, acolytes of the masters learn the unique song to the one they serve, and can sing portions of it to stir the ancient wizards. The act of stirring a Grandmaster does drain at one's soul, shortening one's own life, though the knowledge that can be gained from such a figure is often seen as beyond worthwhile to the acolytes.

Usually, only a tiny fraction of the master's song is sung, and they stir only slightly. In this state they are often rambling and unfocused, with acolytes taking notes for any fragment of wisdom. To truly awaken a Grandmaster requires a choir of powerful wizards to work in unison to sing the song, an act that is likely to kill all involved. However, a fully awakened Grandmaster is a mighty force indeed, able to sweep aside entire armies on their own.

Distantly Jovial

The Scraedai tend to be logical and unbiased in their approach to life. However, despite their apparent coldness, most find the Scraedai quite agreeable. They are usually curious, quick to laughter and eager to discuss with others, though their casual acceptance of necromancy and dismissal of strict morality does put some off.

Debate is particularly beloved among the Scraedai, and heated disagreement is rarely taken as a personal insult. A Scraedai scholar may spend hours ruthlessly dismantling another's life's work, cheerfully admit defeat when their own argument is disproven, and then invite their opponent home for dinner. This can make interactions with less academically minded bugs difficult, as the Scraedai do not always realize when what they consider friendly debate has become an argument.

Academic gossip is similarly common. New theories, failed experiments, embarrassing lectures and disputes between famous scholars can spread across the Whistlewood remarkably quickly. Few things excite a group of Scraedai quite like learning that two respected Grandmasters who despised one another in life have published contradictory opinions several centuries after both of them died.

Scholars and Sages

While most Scraedai are content to live and die within the confines of their libraries and laboratories, there are plenty who seek knowledge from the wider world. Whether out of simple curiosity, to test some esoteric theory or to investigate a strange magical phenomenon, Scraedai are not uncommon to see far from their homelands.

Many view adventuring as little more than particularly dangerous field research. Ancient ruins hold forgotten magic, distant cultures possess knowledge unavailable in the Whistlewood, and monsters provide all manner of opportunities for experimentation. A Scraedai may travel halfway across Llorelan to prove a seemingly insignificant academic point, recover a single lost tome or simply investigate something they found interesting.

Whatever their reasons for leaving, Scraedai adventurers tend to share the curiosity of their people. There is always another mystery to unravel, another theory to test and another question whose answer will inevitably produce several more.

Scraedai Traits

Regardless of what form they take, all Scraedai share the following traits.

  • Ability Score Increase. When determining your character's ability scores, increase one score by 2 and increase a different score by 1, or increase three different scores by 1. You can't raise any of your scores above 20.
  • Creature Type. You are an insectoid.
  • Size. Your size is medium. Scraedai tend to be squat and broad, ranging between 4 and 5 sprigs tall, and weigh up to 250 pebblets.
  • Speed. Your walking speed is 30 sprigs.
  • Darkvision. You can see in dim light within 60 sprigs of you as if it were bright light and in darkness as if it were dim light. You discern colors in that darkness only as shades of gray.
  • Fey Ancestry. You have advantage on saving throws you make to avoid or end the charmed condition on yourself.
  • Sonic Resistance. You have resistance to thunder damage.
  • Sonomancy Training. You know the Alter Sound cantrip. Once you reach 3rd level you can cast Thunderwave. Once you reach 5th level, you can also cast Shatter. Once you cast either spell with this trait, you cannot cast that spell with it again until you finish a long rest; however, you can cast the spell using any Spell Slots you have of the appropriate level.
    • Intelligence, Wisdom or Charisma is your spellcasting ability for the spells you cast with this trait. You choose which ability when you choose this lineage.
  • Wings. Starting at 5th level, you can use a bonus action to magically strengthen your wings for 1 minute. For the duration, you gain a fly speed equal to your walking speed and can hover. Once you use this trait, you can't do so again until yout complete a long rest.
  • Languages. Your character can speak, read and write Click-clack and Scraedai. Scraedai is a language of long, droning syllables, with context and nuance given though the use of sonomantic magic.
