Psionics

When you reach 3rd level, you augment your martial prowess with the ability to use psionics. Psionics is a special form of magic use, distinct from spellcasting, made up of practices instead of spells. A psionic practice cannot be affected by abilities that target spells, such as Counterspell, but are still affected by abilities that target magic in general, such as Dispel Magic.

Psionic Talents

A psionic talent is a minor psionic practice you have mastered. At 1st level, you know two psionic talents of your choice. You learn an additional talent of your choice at 15th level. You chose your talents from the Psionic Talents list.

Psionic Disciplines

A psionic discipline is a rigid set of mental exercises that allows a practitioner to manifest psionic power. A Lurk masters only a few disciplines at a time, and choses them from the Psionic Discipline list.

At 3rd level, you know two psionic disciplines of your choice that are from the Awakened or Nomad mystic orders. You learn an additional discipline at 10th level.

Psi Points

You have an internal reservoir of energy that can be devoted to psionic disciplines you know. This energy is represented by your Psi points. Each psionic discipline describes a list of practices you can create with it by spending psi points. A psionic talent requires no psi points.

The number of psi points you have is based on your rogue level, as shown in the Psi Points column of the Lurk Psionics table. Whenever you complete a long rest, you recover all expended psi points.

Psi Limit

Though you have access to a potent amount of psionic energy, it takes training and practice to channel that energy. There is a limit on the number of psi points you can spend on psionic practices each turn. The limit is based on your rogue level, as shown in the Psi Limit column of the Lurk Psionics table. For example, as a 7th-level rogue, you can spend no more than 3 psi points on practices each turn, no matter how many psi points you have.

Psionic Restrictions

You cannot use psionic talents nor spend psi points on psionic practices while wearing armor or using a shield that you are not proficient with.

Psionic Ability

Intelligence is your psionic ability for your psionic practices. You use your Intelligence modifier when setting the saving throw DC for a psionic practice or when making an attack roll with one.

Psionic save DC = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your Intelligence modifier

Psionic attack modifier = your proficiency bonus + your Intelligence modifier

Psionic Misdirection

When you choose this archetype at 3rd level, you learn the Delusion psionic talent. It doesn't count against the number of psionic talents you know.

Additionally, you can use Delusion as a bonus action instead of an action.

Psychic Siphon

By 7th level you have learned how to drain those you attack of psychic energy, empowering yourself and leaving them open for attack. When you deal Sneak Attack damage to a creature, you may choose to recover a number of spent psi points equal to your psi limit. When you do so, the target has disadvantage on the next saving throw it makes against one of your psionic practices before the end of your next turn.

Once you use this feature, you must complete a long rest before you can use it again.

Distracting Delusions

Starting at 13th level, when you use Delusion on a creature, you have advantage on attack rolls against that creature until the end of the current turn.

Presence Erasure

At 17th level, when you deal Sneak Attack damage to a creature, you can attempt to erase your presence from it's mind. The creature must make an Intelligence saving throw against your psionic save DC. On a failed save, you erase your presence from the target's conscious perception for 1 minute. During that time, the target cannot perceive you through any of its senses. In addition, its mind refuses to recognize your influence on its surroundings. It does not perceive you as the source of any actions you take, and it cannot attribute changes it observes to you. For example, if you take an object, the target perceives the object as simply disappearing, and if you harm another creature, the target perceives the harm but not you as its cause.

The target can still perceive the consequences of your actions and can draw conclusions from them. Other creatures can tell it about you and your actions, but doing so does not allow the target to perceive you.

This effect ends early if you deal damage to the creature or force it to make a saving throw.

On a successful save, the creature is immune to this feature for 24 hours. Once a creature fails its saving throw against this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a long rest.
