Variant Rule: Lifestyles

In standard 5e DnD, there is not much incentive to take care of yourself. Why sleep in a castle when roughing it in a tent provides the same benefits? The following variant rule adds a bit more complexity to this system.

Inspiration: Shadowrun.

Lifestyles

Lifestyles are an abstract measure of wealth, encapsulating housing, income, savings and more. When using this variant rule you must spend coin in order to maintain your lifestyle. Choose one of the lifestyle levels below. Each lifestyle level has a monthly cost you must spend in order to maintain it. If you can no longer support a given lifestyle, you no longer gain its benefits and must choose another lifestyle you can afford.

Complications

Downtime can have risks all to its self. Whenever a player has a significant amount of downtime (roughly a month for medium paced games or as little as a week for fast paced ones), they roll the Complication Die associated with their lifestyle. On a 1 the experience a complication. If a player had more than one lifestyle during a given period, they roll the smallest die.

Complications are meant to be small but meaningful setbacks, such as having an item stolen, suffering a minor injury or delaying a project. They should provide reasonable penalties to players, but should usually be able to be circumvented by expending time and resources.

Lifestyles
Lifestyle Monthly Cost Description Comp. Die
Wretched Free You live in inhumane conditions. With no place to call home, you take shelter where you can find it. Poor access to food and clean water leaves you begging for scraps. Disease, violence and hunger are all commonplace for you. d2
Squalid 3 gp You have some shelter, such as a leaky stable, mud floored hut or a vermin infested boarding house. Hunger, disease and violence are still commonplace for you. d3
Poor 15 gp You live in sufficient, if uncomfortable, conditions. With access to simple food and lodging such as a flophouse or a common room above a tavern, you are protected from the elements and starvation, but poor nutrition and disease still impact you regularly. d4
Modest 30 gp You can ensure you are out of the slums and that you can maintain your equipment. You live in an older part of town renting a room in a boarding house, inn or temple. You get enough food and your living conditions are clean if simple. d6
Comfortable 90 gp You can afford nicer living conditions, food and clothing. You live in a small cottage in a middle class neighborhood or a private room in an inn. d8
Luxurious 300 gp You live in luxury, though not quite to the point of nobility. You have access to high quality food and respectable lodgings such as a spacious home in a good part of town or a suite in a fine inn. You likely have a small staff of servants. d10
Opulent 750 gp You live a life of plenty and comfort. You never go hungry nor thirsty, and have access to the best food and lodging your community can provide. Large homes, fine dining, skilled tailors, expansive retinues of servants attending your every need. You have access to the richest and most powerful people around, such as politicians, guild leaders, high priests and nobility (and all the treachery that comes with them.) d20
