Home

Lifestyles: Homeowning

In standard 5e DnD, there is not much incentive to take care of yourself. Why sleep in a castle when roughing it in a tent provides the same benefits? The following variant rule adds a bit more complexity to this system.

Inspiration: Shadowrun.

Homeowning

Up to this point it is assumed you are renting a space to live as part of your lifestyle cost. However, owning a home can drastically save money, if you can make the upfront cost. While you live in a house, it reduces the monthly cost of it's lifestyle level or lower, as shown in the New Monthly Cost column of the Homes table. If you have a wealth level that already provides the same lifestyle as a house, the base cost of that house is halved for you.

Buying and Building

When buying a house, you can either purchase one or build one, provided there is a house or land for sale (this may also be limited by a local lord or other authority.) When building, the cost is reduced but house cannot be lived in until the construction time elapses. This time is paused during winters or other periods where construction cannot happen.

The price of a house is decided by its associated lifestyle and the number of rooms. A house costs its base price, plus the number of additional rooms x its room cost. Even without additional rooms, a house has enough space and furnishing for two medium creatures to live.

Utility Buildings

Sometimes you don't need all the bells and whistles included in a finished house, sometimes four walls and a roof is enough. These utility buildings do not protect from external temperatures or humidity and as such are not fit for living in. That being said, they are cheap and easy to get. Any upgrade thats use would be effected by being in an unfinished space cannot be built in a utility building.

Land

Homeowners oftentimes own land surrounding their home. Each home has a number of land upgrade slots, which can be used to build special upgrades. The cost of land is dependent on a home's locale (see below.)

Locale

Where a home is greatly effects costs and build times. A home can be in an Remote, Rural or Urban locale, with the following benefits and penalties for each:

Remote. A Remote locale is far away from towns and cities, safe from prying eyes and meddlesome authorities. Examples include harsh mountaintops, deep in forested wilderness, or far in a hostile desert. Land in a Remote locale costs 25 gp per slot. Additionally, the cost to build, buy or upgrade the home is increased by 50%, and the build times are doubled, as the increased privacy and costs to ship in or source materials raise the costs.

Rural. A Rural locale is outside of a city or town, but close enough to make a day trip of it. Examples include a quiet countryside of farmlands, a forest just out of town, or an island just outside of a port city. Land in a Rural locale costs 50 gp per slot. The costs and build time for the house are unchanged.

Urban. An Urban locale is directly within the bounds of a city or other settlement. Examples include a small townhouse in a major city, a manor with a small yard and wall in an affluent neighborhood, or a hovel near the city's docks. Land in a Urban locale costs 100 gp per slot, and automatically have the clear land upgrade. Additionally, the time to build the house is reduced by half, as getting access to materials and laborers is quite simple.

House Upgrades

Upon purchasing, each house contains a common room, a place to cook, eat and sleep. Any additional rooms purchased are unfurnished. Several House Upgrades are specialized rooms that can fill these additional rooms, provided it meets the upgrade's requirements and you pay the costs. Each upgrade takes up a number of rooms, if you do not have enough empty rooms, you cannot select an upgrade. Each upgrade can only be used once it is completed, which takes the amount of time specified.
